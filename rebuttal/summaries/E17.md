# E17: DL3DV second method — splatfacto/3DGS (Task P17)

**Status: RUNNING, 4/140 scenes at last check, zero failures.**
**CAMERA-READY MATERIAL ONLY — nothing in the posted rebuttal may cite this,
and the audit is not yet computed (`E17.audit` is null).**

## Setup

The E13 streamed design with nerfacto swapped for splatfacto: `ns-train
splatfacto` defaults, `--machine.seed 0`, downscale 4 (960P), COLMAP-seeded
Gaussian initialization, per-scene PSNR/SSIM/LPIPS in the E13 log schema
with a provenance field marking it camera-ready. Resumable; scenes are
claimed atomically on the shared filesystem so heterogeneous GPUs
self-balance from one queue, and a failed scene releases its claim.

Running on a 2-GPU node (H100 NVL 94 GB + H200 NVL 140 GB), 6 workers.

## Measured performance

17.9–18.0 min/scene/worker **including** ns-eval; ~3 min/scene effective
with 6 workers, so ~7 h for 140 scenes. The bottleneck is the scheduler's
16-CPU-per-node cap on the GPU partition (splatfacto undistorts ~381 images
per scene on CPU), **not** the GPUs: 7 GB of 94/140 GB used, and adding a
third worker per GPU left aggregate throughput flat (~15k steps/min either
way). The partition with 112 CPUs permits zero GPUs, so no configuration on
that cluster lifts the cap.

## Honest expectation for the two-method audit

The between-method gaps (~4–5 dB PSNR, 0.16–0.22 SSIM) are far larger than
the DL3DV tolerances (tau_PSNR = 0.2014 dB under the E3c rule), so the
two-method joint event is a genuinely demanding test: a subset must track
two very differently-behaved distributions at once. Expect a certified
budget **at or above** nerfacto's 32 scenes. To be reported either way.

## AUDIT COMPLETE — two-method DL3DV joint event (CAMERA-READY ONLY)

**Not for the discussion-period rebuttal.** 140/140 splatfacto logs, zero
failures. Contract = the E13 contract extended method-wise: same k=4 regimes,
per-method E3c tolerances (c = 0.079769 x that method's *own* per-scene std),
same size-dependent KS guardrail, M=400, seed 0. A subset passes only if
**both** methods satisfy all six constraints simultaneously (12 constraints
total). Validation gate reproduced 88/400, LCB 0.1822 first.

Populations: nerfacto PSNR 22.914 +- 2.525, splatfacto 30.381 +- 2.865.
Note the dispersion rule automatically tightens the better method's
constraints — splatfacto's SSIM tolerance is 0.0032 vs nerfacto's 0.0094,
because splatfacto is more uniformly good.

| Scenes | 8 | 12 | 16 | 20 | 24 | 32 | 40 | 48 | 64 | 80 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Equal | 1 | 3 | 2 | 6 | 2 | 9 | 5 | 5 | 5 | 2 |
| Proportional | 0 | 1 | 1 | 6 | 12 | 23 | **47 (.090)** | 70 (.141) | 119 (.255) | 180 (.402) |

- **Equal allocation fails at every budget** (best LCB 0.012). Even starker
  than the single-method case — the equal-allocation bias compounds when
  several methods must be satisfied at once.
- **Proportional certifies 40 scenes at p_min = 0.08** (47/400, LCB 0.0895)
  and 64 scenes at p_min = 0.20.

### The audit cost scales with method count — and the claim survives

| Methods | p_min = 0.08 | p_min = 0.20 |
|---|---|---|
| 1 (nerfacto, E13) | 32 scenes | 40 scenes |
| 2 (+ splatfacto) | **40 scenes** | **64 scenes** |

Adding a second method raises the certified budget by 25% at the default
target — a real cost, honestly reported, but the compact-subset claim
survives comfortably (40 of 140 is still a 3.5x reduction). This is the
quantitative answer to "how does the protocol scale with the number of
methods audited", and it is consistent with E1/E10 on Nutrition5k, where
equal allocation also failed and proportional was the fix.

## THREE-METHOD AUDIT (nerfacto + splatfacto + tensorf) — CAMERA-READY ONLY

tensorf: 140/140, zero failures, PSNR 23.122 +- 2.684 (tau 0.2141). All three
methods now under one harness (nerfstudio, 30k iters, seed 0, downscale 4,
ns-eval). 18 simultaneous constraints. Validation gate reproduced 88/400 first.

| Scenes | 24 | 32 | 40 | 48 | 64 | 80 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Equal | 2 | 0 | 2 | 2 | 5 | 2 |
| Proportional | 3 | 8 | 14 | 36 | **67 (.134)** | 113 (.241) |

- **Equal allocation fails everywhere** (best LCB 0.0054) — as with two methods.
- **Proportional certifies 64 scenes at p_min = 0.08** and 80 at p_min = 0.20.

### Audit cost vs method count — the headline scaling result

| Methods | p_min = 0.08 | p_min = 0.20 | vs full 140 |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 (nerfacto) | 32 scenes | 40 scenes | 4.4x reduction |
| 2 (+ splatfacto) | 40 scenes | 64 scenes | 3.5x |
| 3 (+ tensorf) | **64 scenes** | **80 scenes** | **2.2x** |

The certified budget grows roughly linearly in the number of methods audited
(32 -> 40 -> 64), and the compact-subset claim still holds at three methods,
though the margin is narrowing: 64 of 140 is a 2.2x reduction, versus 4.4x
for a single method. Extrapolating, a fourth method would likely need ~80-100
of 140 scenes, at which point "compact" stops being meaningful for a
population this small. That boundary is itself the useful finding: **the
achievable compression is set by how many methods must be certified jointly
and by the population size**, not by the protocol.

tensorf is also the useful third method precisely because it is *not*
redundant: it sits near nerfacto on PSNR (23.12 vs 22.91) but is much worse
on LPIPS (0.340 vs 0.166), so it constrains the perceptual dimension
independently rather than restating what nerfacto already says.

## FOUR-METHOD AUDIT (+ instant-ngp) — CAMERA-READY ONLY

instant-ngp: 140/140, zero failures. **Trained to 16,000 steps, NOT the 30,000
default used by the other three methods** (NVlabs' own run.py falls back to
35,000). Chosen because measured marginal train-PSNR gain per 2k steps drops
below the audit tolerance at ~14-16k. Every log records `train_steps: 16000`.
The audit stays valid because all constraints are within-method, but **no fair
cross-method quality comparison may be drawn against the other three.**

Population: PSNR 24.558 +- 2.367 (tau 0.1888). 24 simultaneous constraints.

| Scenes | 24 | 32 | 40 | 48 | 64 | 80 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Equal | 1 | 0 | 1 | 2 | 3 | 0 |
| Proportional | 2 | 5 | 12 | 19 | **56 (.109)** | 106 (.224) |

### The scaling curve SATURATES — my earlier extrapolation was wrong

| Methods | p_min = 0.08 | p_min = 0.20 | vs full 140 |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 (nerfacto) | 32 | 40 | 4.4x |
| 2 (+ splatfacto) | 40 | 64 | 3.5x |
| 3 (+ tensorf) | 64 | 80 | 2.2x |
| 4 (+ instant-ngp) | **64** | **80** | **2.2x** |

I predicted a fourth method would need ~80-100 scenes. It needs **64** — the
same as three. The fourth method does tighten the event (pass count at 64
scenes falls 67 -> 56) but not enough to move the certified budget. So the
cost of adding methods is **sublinear and saturating**, not linear: the
compact-subset claim is more robust to method count than the 32 -> 40 -> 64
trend suggested. Equal allocation still fails at every budget for all
multi-method events.

### The under-training worry did NOT materialise

A concern raised before the 16k run: under-training could inflate per-scene
variance, loosening tau (= 0.0798 sigma) and flattering the audit. The
measured stds refute it — instant-ngp has the **smallest** sigma_PSNR of the
four (2.367 vs nerfacto 2.525, tensorf 2.684, splatfacto 2.865), hence the
**tightest** tolerance (0.1888). Its constraints were the strictest, not the
loosest, so the four-method result is not flattered by the shortened schedule.

## Disclosure required wherever the four-method result is used

1. instant-ngp trained at 16k steps vs 30k for nerfacto/splatfacto/tensorf and
   35k in NVlabs' reference implementation.
2. Valid for the within-method audit and the scaling curve; NOT valid for
   ranking method quality.
3. All four share the harness otherwise: nerfstudio, seed 0, downscale 4
   (960P), ns-eval metrics, identical k=4 regimes and E3c tolerance rule.
